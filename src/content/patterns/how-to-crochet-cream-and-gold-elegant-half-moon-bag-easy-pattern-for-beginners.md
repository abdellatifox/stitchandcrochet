## What You'll Need
Here's everything you’ll need to bring this gorgeous bag to life:

- **Yarn:** Approximately 250 yards of worsted weight cotton or cotton blend yarn in cream or off-white. This medium-thickness yarn is easy to see while you crochet and gives a beautiful stitch definition.
  
- **Crochet Hook:** 4.0mm (G/6) crochet hook. If your tension runs loose, choose a smaller size to ensure the bag is sturdy.

- **Stitch Markers (optional but helpful):** These help mark the beginning of your rounds or specific stitches to keep track of your progress.

- **Scissors:** For cutting your yarn at the end of a project.

- **Yarn/Tapestry Needle:** This needle is used for weaving in the ends of your yarn.

- **Gold Metal Ring Handle:** One 5-inch size to create the elegant look of your bag.

- **Gold Chain Strap:** Approximately 45 inches, allowing for a crossbody or shoulder bag style.

- **Lobster Clasp Attachments:** Two for attaching the chain strap to your bag.

- **D-Rings or Jump Rings:** Two for attaching your chain strap securely.

- **Small Decorative Bow Charm (optional):** Adds a cute touch to your bag.

- **Fabric for Lining (optional but recommended):** Gives your bag a finished look.

## Stitches You'll Use in This Pattern
Here’s a breakdown of the stitches you'll need to know:

- **ch = chain:** Yarn over (wrap yarn around the hook), pull through the loop on your hook. This creates the starting point for your work.

- **sl st = slip stitch:** Insert your hook into the stitch, yarn over, pull through both the stitch and the loop on your hook in one motion. This joins stitches or moves you over without adding height.

- **sc = single crochet:** Insert your hook into the stitch, yarn over, pull up a loop (you now have 2 loops on your hook), yarn over again, and pull through both loops. This is a very basic stitch.

- **sc2tog = single crochet two together:** Insert your hook into the first stitch and pull up a loop. Then, insert your hook into the next stitch, pull up another loop (you now have 3 loops on your hook). Yarn over and pull through all 3 loops. This decreases your stitch count.

- **bob = bobble stitch:** Yarn over, insert your hook, pull up a loop, yarn over, pull through 2 loops. Repeat this step 4 more times in the same stitch. You will have 6 loops on your hook. Yarn over and pull through all 6 loops, then gently push the bobble to the front of your work.

- **inc = increase:** Work 2 stitches into the same stitch to increase your stitch count.

- **BLO = back loop only:** Instead of inserting your hook under both loops at the top of the stitch, go only under the back loop. This will create a ridged texture.

## Special Stitches
### Working Bobbles on Right Side Rows
To create an effective bobble, always ensure that you gently push it to the front of your work as you complete it. This will help it pop into the textured shape you desire. Don't worry if they look messy at first; they will come together beautifully!

### Creating the Half Moon Shape
We achieve the crescent shape by working increases at certain points while keeping the central part the same. Think of it as creating a smile!

## Let's Get Started: Full Pattern Instructions
Now, let's jump into the detailed instructions for making the half moon bag!

### Panel One (Make 2)
#### Foundation Row:
1. **Ch 45**. 
   
#### Row 1 (RS):
2. **Sc in 2nd ch from hook and in each ch across.** Turn. (44 sts)

#### Row 2:
3. **Ch 1, sc in BLO across.** Turn. (44 sts)

#### Row 3 (Bobble Row):
4. **Ch 1, sc in first 2 sts,** *bob in next st, sc in next 3 sts* repeat from * to last 2 sts, **sc in last 2 sts**. Turn. (44 sts, 10 bobbles)

#### Row 4:
5. **Ch 1, sc in BLO across.** Turn. (44 sts)

#### Row 5:
6. **Ch 1, sc across.** Turn. (44 sts)

#### Row 6:
7. **Ch 1, sc in BLO across.** Turn. (44 sts)

#### Row 7 (Bobble Row):
8. **Ch 1, sc in first 4 sts,** *bob in next st, sc in next 3 sts* repeat from * to last 4 sts, **bob in next st, sc in last 3 sts**. Turn. (44 sts, 10 bobbles offset from Row 3)

#### Row 8:
9. **Ch 1, sc in BLO across.** Turn. (44 sts)

#### Rows 9-10:
10. **Repeat Rows 5-6.** (44 sts)

#### Rows 11-18:
11. **Repeat Rows 3-10.** (44 sts)

### Now we begin shaping the curve.

#### Row 19:
12. **Ch 1, sc2tog, sc to last 2 sts, sc2tog.** Turn. (42 sts)

#### Row 20:
13. **Ch 1, sc in BLO across.** Turn. (42 sts)

#### Row 21 (Bobble Row):
14. **Ch 1, sc2tog, sc in next st,** *bob in next st, sc in next 3 sts* repeat to last 5 sts, **bob in next st, sc in next 2 sts, sc2tog.** Turn. (40 sts)

#### Row 22:
15. **Ch 1, sc in BLO across.** Turn. (40 sts)

#### Row 23:
16. **Ch 1, sc2tog, sc to last 2 sts, sc2tog.** Turn. (38 sts)

#### Row 24:
17. **Ch 1, sc in BLO across.** Turn. (38 sts)

#### Row 25 (Bobble Row):
18. **Ch 1, sc2tog,** *bob in next st, sc in next 3 sts* repeat to last 4 sts, **bob in next st, sc in next st, sc2tog.** Turn. (36 sts)

#### Row 26:
19. **Ch 1, sc in BLO across.** Turn. (36 sts)

#### Row 27:
20. **Ch 1, sc2tog twice, sc to last 4 sts, sc2tog twice.** Turn. (32 sts)

#### Row 28:
21. **Ch 1, sc in BLO across.** Turn. (32 sts)

#### Row 29:
22. **Ch 1, sc2tog twice, sc to last 4 sts, sc2tog twice.** Turn. (28 sts)

#### Row 30:
23. **Ch 1, sc in BLO across.** Turn. (28 sts)

#### Row 31:
24. **Ch 1, sc2tog three times, sc to last 6 sts, sc2tog three times.** Turn. (22 sts)

#### Row 32:
25. **Ch 1, sc in BLO across. Fasten off, leaving a long tail.** (22 sts)

*This creates that beautiful curved top edge.*

### Joining the Panels
26. Place both panels with wrong sides together. The bobbles should face outward on both sides.
27. Starting at one top corner, work **sc evenly around the bottom curved edge only,** going through both layers to join. Work approximately 3 sc per 2 rows along the sides and 1 sc per stitch along the bottom.
28. When you reach the other top corner, fasten off. The top opening should remain unjoined.

### Finishing the Top Edge and Handle Attachment
#### Top Border
29. Join yarn at one top corner.
30. **Round 1:** **Ch 1, sc evenly around** the entire top opening, working through single layer. Place a stitch marker at the center back. Join with **sl st.** (approximately 88-92 sts)

31. **Round 2:** **Ch 1, sc in each st to center back marker, ch 10 for handle loop, skip 8 sts, sc to end.** Join.

32. **Round 3:** **Ch 1, sc around,** working 10 sc into the ch-10 space. Join. Fasten off.
*If you want both attachment points, repeat the handle loop creation on the front panel.*

### Attaching the Gold Ring Handle
33. Thread the top edge through your gold ring handle. The yarn-wrapped section visible in the photo comes from wrapping the ring with yarn before attaching.
34. To wrap the handle: Cut approximately 3 yards of yarn. Starting at one end of the wrap section, hold the tail against the ring and begin wrapping yarn tightly around both the ring and the tail. Continue for about 2.5 inches. 
35. Thread the end through a tapestry needle and weave under several wraps to secure. Trim excess.
36. Fold the top border over the ring and whip stitch the edge to the inside of the bag.

### Adding the Chain Strap
37. Attach D-rings to each upper corner of the bag. You can sew them directly to the corner stitches or create small crochet loops to hold them.
*To make a small loop (optional):* Ch 8, sl st to form a ring, sc 12 into the ring, sl st to join. Thread through D-ring and sew to bag corner.
38. Clip your gold chain to the D-rings using the lobster clasps.

### Creating the Tassel
*Your bag can shine even more with a tassel!*
1. Cut a piece of cardboard about 5 inches tall. Wrap yarn around it approximately 60 times.
2. Thread a 12-inch piece of yarn under all wraps at the top. Tie tightly.
3. Cut the bottom loops.
4. About 1 inch from the top, wrap another piece of yarn around all strands several times. Tie securely and hide the ends inside the tassel.
5. Trim the bottom evenly.
6. Attach to your bag at one corner using the top tie.

### Optional Embellishments
You can add a decorative bow charm for a touch of glam. Simply sew or clip it to the center front, just below the handle. If you'd like added structure, consider using a lining. Cut your fabric slightly smaller than your bag dimensions, sew side and bottom seams, and hand stitch inside along the top edge.

## Troubleshooting Tips
- **Bobbles not popping?** Make sure you are pushing them to the front as you work. Also check that you are completing all 5 yarn overs before pulling through.
- **Bag too floppy?** Go down a hook size. Tighter tension is key for better structure for bags.
- **Panels different sizes?** Block them before joining. Wet blocking cotton works beautifully.
- **Curved edges not matching?** Count your decreases. Each panel should decrease the same number of stitches on each row.
