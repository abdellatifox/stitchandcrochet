Creating your very own hanging scarf organizer is easier than you might think! This project is beginner-friendly and will help you keep your scarves neat and accessible while also adding a stylish touch to your room or closet. Let’s get started on making your own Scarf Ladder!

## What You'll Need
- **Yarn:**
  - Weight Category: **Worsted (Medium)** — This is a medium-thickness yarn that is easy to work with and helps you see your stitches clearly.
  - Fiber Content: **100% cotton** — This gives your scarf organizer a nice texture and durability.
  - **Main Color (MC):** Turquoise (approximately 250 yards)
  - **Contrast Color (CC):** Off-white (approximately 150 yards)
  
- **Other Materials:**
  - **12 wooden dowels, each 12 inches long (30.5 cm)** — These will hold your scarf loops.
  - **1 wooden or metal hook for hanging (optional)** — Use this to hang your organizer up.

- **Crochet Hook:** 
  - **Size:** 5.00 mm (H-8 US) — This is the hook size you'll use for the project.

- **Other Tools:**
  - **Tapestry needle** — This is for weaving in the ends.
  - **Scissors** — To cut your yarn.
  - **Measuring tape** — To check measurements as you work.
  - **Stitch markers (optional)** — These can help you keep track of your rows.

## Stitches You'll Use in This Pattern
- **Chain (CH):** To create the starting base, yarn over (yarn going over your hook) and pull through the loop on your hook.
- **Double Crochet (DC):** Yarn over, insert your hook into a stitch, yarn over again and pull back through. You’ll have three loops on your hook. Yarn over again, pull through the first two loops, then yarn over and pull through the last two loops.
- **Slip Stitch (SL ST):** Insert your hook into a stitch, yarn over, and pull the yarn through both the stitch and the loop on your hook. This is used mainly for joining pieces.
- **Single Crochet (SC):** Insert your hook into a stitch, yarn over, pull up a loop, yarn over again, and pull through both loops on your hook.
  
*Don't worry if these stitches feel tricky at first! With practice, you'll get the hang of them.*

## Let's Get Started: Full Pattern Instructions

### Body
#### Foundation Row
1. **CH 50** (50 sts)
   - This means you will create 50 chain stitches to start.

#### Row 1
2. **DC in 4th CH from hook, DC in each CH across** (48 sts)
   - To do this, count 1, 2, 3, 4 in your chain and work a double crochet in the 4th chain. Then continue doing a double crochet in each chain until you reach the end. 

#### Row 2
3. **CH 3 (counts as first DC), turn. DC in each DC across** (48 sts)
   - Here, chain 3 to count as your first double crochet, then turn your work. Perform a double crochet in each double crochet across the row.

#### Repeat Row 2
4. **Continue to repeat Row 2** until your piece measures approximately 36 inches (91.5 cm) long. You should end with a total of 48 double crochets after your last row.

#### Edging
5. **CH 1, SC in each ST across** (48 sts)
   - Chain 1, then make a single crochet in each stitch across to create a neat edge.
   - **Fasten off** your work and use your tapestry needle to weave in any ends.

### Loops
#### Make 12 Loops
1. **CH 30** (30 sts)
   - Create a chain of 30 for each loop.

2. **SC in 2nd CH from hook, SC in each CH across** (29 sts)
   - Skip the first chain, then do a single crochet in each chain until the end of your 30 chains.
   - **Fasten off**, leaving a long tail for sewing.
   
3. **Repeat** for a total of 12 loops.

#### Attach Loops to Body
1. **Evenly space the loops along the bottom edge of the body.**
   - You will want to place them about 3 inches (7.5 cm) apart. 

2. **Using the tapestry needle and the long tail, sew the bottom ends of each loop securely onto the body.** 
   - Make sure they are attached tightly.

### Assembly / Putting It All Together
#### Final Assembly
1. **Attach the wooden dowels at intervals along the top edge.** 
   - This adds stability to your scarf organizer.

2. **Fold the top edge of the body over each dowel.**
   - This creates a pocket where the dowels will sit. Sew or slip stitch this pocket closed securely.

#### Hanging
3. **Use one of the dowels to attach your hanging hook or securely fasten the top dowel directly to the wall or closet.**
   - This way, your scarf ladder will hang safely.

4. **Weave in any loose ends from the loops and body.**
   - Use your tapestry needle for this, ensuring everything looks neat.

### Finishing
- **Weave in Ends:** To weave in ends, thread the end of your yarn onto your tapestry needle and weave it through several stitches on the back of your project. This hides the yarn so it doesn’t unravel.

- **Blocking (optional):** Blocking is when you shape your crochet piece and allow it to dry to keep a nice form. If you want your scarf ladder to look extra polished, you can lightly stretch it into shape and pin it down on a flat surface until it dries.
