Welcome to this fun crochet project! Today, I'll walk you through making a cozy couch arm remote caddy. This handy caddy will help keep your remotes, snacks, and small items organized and within reach. Don't worry if this is your first crochet project — I've made sure this pattern is beginner-friendly!

## What You'll Need
- **Yarn:**
  - Worsted Weight (#4) Acrylic Yarn
    - Main Color (MC): Teal (approx. 200 yards) — this medium-thickness yarn is easy to work with and see your stitches.
    - Contrast Color (CC): Light Grey (approx. 100 yards) — a great way to add a little flair to your project.
- **Crochet Hook:** 
  - 5.5 mm (I-9) crochet hook — this is the size you need to create the right stitch tension.
- **Stitch Markers (optional but helpful):** 
  - These help keep track of your rows or stitches.
- **Scissors:** 
  - For cutting your yarn.
- **Yarn/Tapestry Needle:** 
  - This needle helps you weave in the loose ends of your yarn for a neat finish.

## Stitches You'll Use in This Pattern
- **Chain (Ch):** This creates the little loops that start your work. To make a chain, simply make a loop with your yarn and pull the tail through. 
- **Double Crochet (DC):** This stitch gives your fabric a nice height and texture. To do a double crochet, yarn over, insert your hook into the stitch, yarn over again, pull through (3 loops on hook), yarn over, pull through the first 2 loops (2 loops left), yarn over, and pull through the last 2 loops.
- **Single Crochet (SC):** A tighter stitch that's great for edges. Insert your hook into the stitch, yarn over, pull through (2 loops on hook), yarn over, pull through both loops.
- **Slip Stitch (SL ST):** Used for joining rounds or moving in your work. Insert your hook into the stitch, yarn over, and pull through both the stitch and loop on hook.
- **Stitch (St):** This simply refers to any stitch in crochet.

## Let's Get Started: Full Pattern Instructions

### Base
#### Round 1
1. **Ch 42** (41 stitches + 1 additional for turning).
2. **Row 1:** DC in 4th ch from hook (this will be your first DC), then DC in each ch across. Turn your work. **(40 DC)**
3. **Row 2:** Ch 3 (this counts as your first DC), then DC in each of the next 39 DC across your row. Turn. **(40 DC)**
4. **Row 3-12:** For each of these rows, repeat Row 2. After you complete Row 12, you'll have **(40 DC)** in each row.

### Pockets (Make 2)
- **Using Contrast Color**
#### Round 1
1. **Ch 25** (24 stitches + 1 additional for turning).
2. **Row 1:** DC in 4th ch from hook, then DC in each ch across. Turn. **(24 DC)**
3. **Row 2:** Ch 3, then DC in each of the next 23 DC across. Turn. **(24 DC)**
4. **Row 3-5:** For each of these rows, repeat Row 2. You will finish with **(24 DC)** in each row.

### Assembly
1. **Position the pockets:** Place one pocket 5 inches from the left edge of the base and the other pocket 5 inches from the right edge. Center the pockets vertically about 5 inches from the bottom of the base.
2. **Attach pockets:** Align the top edges of the pockets with the base. Using the Main Color (Teal), working from the right side, make an SC through the top edge of the pocket and through the base, securing it. Work 24 SC evenly along the top of the pocket.

### Edging
1. Switch to Contrast Color (Light Grey).
2. **Ch 1** and SC around the entire edges of the remote caddy. Make 3 SC in each corner to give it a nice shape.  Finally, join with SL ST to the first SC.
3. Fasten off by cutting the yarn and pulling it through the last loop. Weave in all ends using your tapestry needle.

## Finishing
### Weave in Ends
To weave in ends, thread your tapestry needle with the yarn tail. Pass the needle through several stitches in your finished piece, this will hide the ends and keep them secured.

### Blocking (Optional)
Blocking is a way to shape your finished project and smooth out any uneven edges. To block, wet your project, then lay it flat and shape it to the desired measurements. Let it dry completely.
