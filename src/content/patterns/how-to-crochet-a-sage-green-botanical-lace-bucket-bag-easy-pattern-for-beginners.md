Are you ready to create something beautiful that you can use every day? This Sage Green Botanical Lace Bucket Bag combines a sturdy single crochet base with delicate lace overlay panels. Don't worry, it's beginner-friendly!

## Why You’ll Love This Pattern
This pattern is not just any tote bag project. You'll crochet a solid base first and then create beautiful lace pieces that attach to the bag. This means you can take your time with the lace, and it’ll look like a professional bag in the end. The bag is spacious enough to carry your essentials, making it perfect for outings or everyday use.

## What You'll Need
Gather these supplies to make your bucket bag:
- **Yarn:** 
  - Main Color (MC): Approximately 200 yards of **worsted weight** cotton yarn in forest green or deep sage — a medium thickness that's easy to work with.
  - Contrast Color (CC): Approximately 180 yards of **sport weight** or **DK** cotton yarn in pale sage or mint — lighter weight helps create delicate lace.
  - Tassel Yarn: Additional 30 yards of CC for tassels and drawstring.
- **Crochet Hook:** 
  - 4.0mm (G/6) hook for the main bucket body.
  - 3.5mm (E/4) hook for the lace overlay panels.
  - 3.25mm (D/3) hook for tighter tension on the base (optional).
- **Stitch Markers (optional but helpful):** These help mark the beginning of your rounds, keeping track of where you are.
- **Scissors:** For cutting your yarn.
- **Yarn Needle:** This is used for weaving in the ends of your yarn to tidy up your project.
- **Measuring Tape:** To check your gauge and finished sizes.
- **Fabric Liner (optional but recommended):** For added durability inside the bag.
- **Cardboard Circle for Base Stiffening (optional):** To give the base more structure.

## Stitches You'll Use in This Pattern
Here are the stitches used in this pattern, explained in detail:
- **ch = chain:** Wrap yarn over hook, pull through the loop on your hook to create a little loop. This creates the foundation stitches.
- **sl st = slip stitch:** Insert hook into the stitch, yarn over, pull through both the stitch and the loop on your hook in one motion. Used for joining rounds.
- **sc = single crochet:** Insert hook into the stitch, yarn over, pull up a loop (you’ll have 2 loops on your hook), yarn over again and pull through both loops. This is your basic stitch.
- **hdc = half double crochet:** Yarn over, insert hook, yarn over and pull up a loop (you'll now have 3 loops), yarn over and pull through all 3 loops. It’s slightly taller than single crochet.
- **dc = double crochet:** Yarn over, insert hook, yarn over and pull up a loop (3 loops on your hook), yarn over and pull through 2 loops (you'll have 2 loops remaining), yarn over and pull through the remaining 2 loops. This creates a more open fabric.
- **tr = treble crochet:** Yarn over twice, insert hook, yarn over and pull up a loop (you will have 4 loops), yarn over and pull through 2 loops (you'll have 3 loops), yarn over and pull through 2 more loops (2 loops remain), yarn over and pull through the last 2 loops. This stitch is tall and is used in the lace work.
- **ch-sp = chain space:** This is the gap created by chain stitches in the previous row where you can work into.
- **sk = skip:** Do not work into the indicated number of stitches.
- **inc = increase:** Work 2 stitches into the same stitch to add width.
- **BLO = back loop only:** Insert your hook just under the back loop, which is the loop furthest from you, instead of both loops.

## Let's Get Started: Full Pattern Instructions

### Part 1: Crocheting the Bucket Base
You will create the base using a magic ring and increases in a spiral. Use a stitch marker to mark the start of each round.

**With MC and 4.0mm hook:**
- **Round 1:** Make a magic ring. Chain 1 (this does not count as a stitch), work 6 single crochets (sc) into the ring. Pull the ring closed. (6 stitches)
- **Round 2:** Work 2 sc in each stitch around. (12 stitches)
- **Round 3:** *Sc in next stitch, 2 sc in next stitch* — repeat this around. (18 stitches)
- **Round 4:** *Sc in the next 2 stitches, 2 sc in the next stitch* — repeat around. (24 stitches)
- **Round 5:** *Sc in the next 3 stitches, 2 sc in the next stitch* — repeat around. (30 stitches)
- **Round 6:** *Sc in the next 4 stitches, 2 sc in the next stitch* — repeat around. (36 stitches)
- **Round 7:** *Sc in the next 5 stitches, 2 sc in the next stitch* — repeat around. (42 stitches)
- **Round 8:** *Sc in the next 6 stitches, 2 sc in the next stitch* — repeat around. (48 stitches)
- **Round 9:** *Sc in the next 7 stitches, 2 sc in the next stitch* — repeat around. (54 stitches)
- **Round 10:** *Sc in the next 8 stitches, 2 sc in the next stitch* — repeat around. (60 stitches)

**Your base should now measure approximately 5 inches across.** Lay it flat to check. If it ruffles, your tension is too loose. If it cups, you may need to go up a hook size.

### Part 2: Building the Bucket Sides
To create vertical sides instead of a flat circle, follow the next rounds.

- **Round 11:** Working in the back loop only (BLO), sc in each stitch around. Do not increase. (60 stitches)  
  *This BLO round creates a crisp edge where the base meets the sides.*
  
- **Rounds 12 through 40:** Sc in each stitch around. (60 stitches per round)  
  Continue for a total of 29 rounds of straight sides. This will give you approximately 6.5 inches in height. Ensure the fabric is dense with no visible holes.

**Tip:** Count your stitches every few rounds to ensure you maintain 60 stitches.

- **Round 41:** Slip stitch (sl st) to the first stitch. Fasten off the MC and weave in your ends.

### Part 3: Creating the Lace Overlay Panels
Now, let’s make the beautiful lace pieces! You’ll create 6 identical flower motifs that join together to form the decorative overlay.

**With CC and 3.5mm hook:**
- **Motif Center:**
  - **Round 1:** Magic ring. Chain 3 (counts as first double crochet), work 11 double crochets (dc) into the ring. Slip stitch to the top of the chain-3 to join. (12 dc)
  
  - **Round 2:** Chain 4 (counts as dc + chain 1), *dc in next stitch, chain 1* — repeat around. Slip stitch to the 3rd chain of starting chain-4. (12 dc, 12 chain-1 spaces)
  
  - **Round 3:** Slip stitch into first chain-space. Chain 3, work 2 dc in the same space, chain 2, *3 dc in next chain-space, chain 2* — repeat around. Slip stitch to the top of the chain-3. (12 clusters of 3 dc)

  - **Round 4:** Chain 1, sc in the same stitch, sc in the next 2 dc, *3 sc in chain-2 space, sc in next 3 dc* — repeat around, ending with 3 sc in the final chain-space. Slip stitch to the first sc. (48 sc)

- **Creating the Petal Points:**
  - **Round 5:** Chain 1, sc in the same stitch, *skip 2 stitches, work 5 dc in next stitch (this creates a shell), skip 2 stitches, sc in next stitch* — repeat around. Slip stitch to your first sc. (8 shells)
  
  - **Round 6:** Slip stitch to the center double crochet of the first shell. Chain 4, *treble crochet (tr) in the center double crochet of the next shell, chain 3* — repeat around. Slip stitch to the top of the chain-4.
  
  - **Round 7:** *Work (2 dc, chain 2, 2 dc) in chain-3 space* — repeat around. Slip stitch to the first double crochet. This creates the scalloped edge visible in the finished bag.

**Fasten off, leaving a 12-inch tail for joining.** Make 6 motifs total.

### Part 4: Joining Motifs and Attaching Overlay
Now, let’s join the motifs together to create the lace overlay.

1. Lay the motifs in a row. Using CC, whipstitch or slip stitch the adjacent motifs together at the touching petal points. Connect all 6 into a continuous band.

2. Position the lace band around the top of the bucket body with the scalloped edges pointing down. The band should sit approximately 2 inches below the bucket opening.

3. To attach, use your yarn needle and CC yarn to tack the top edge of each motif to the bucket body with invisible stitches. Work from the inside when possible. This way, the lace overlay drapes beautifully over the solid green base, creating a lovely effect.

### Part 5: The Twisted Rope Handle
This is a fun part! We will use a chaining technique to create the handle.

**With CC and 4.0mm hook:**
1. Chain 150. Fasten off, leaving long tails at both ends.

2. Cut two additional lengths of CC yarn, each 60 inches long. Hold all three strands together (the chain plus the two plain strands) and twist tightly in one direction.

3. Once twisted, fold it in half, allowing the twist to coil back on itself naturally. Secure both ends with tight wrapping. 

4. Attach the handle ends securely inside the bag at opposite sides using your yarn needle and MC.

### Part 6: Creating the Drawstring and Tassels
Let’s finish it up with the drawstring and tassels!

1. **Drawstring:** Chain 100 using CC. You can either twist it like the handle or leave it as a simple chain cord. Weave it through the lace openings around the top of the bag.

2. **Tassels (make two):** 
   - Wrap CC yarn around a 3-inch piece of cardboard 25 times. Tie at the top and cut the bottom loops.
   - Wrap yarn around the tassel 1 inch below the top to create a head, and then trim the ends evenly.
   - Attach the tassels to the drawstring ends.

## Finishing Your Botanical Lace Bucket Bag
Now you’re almost done! Here are the final steps:
- Weave in all remaining ends. 
- Steam block the lace sections gently to open up the stitch definition. Do not press flat.
- **Optional:** Cut a fabric circle to fit the base interior. This prevents stretching and adds polish.
- **Optional:** Insert a thin cardboard circle wrapped in fabric at the very bottom for extra structure.

## Tips for Success
- Working with two yarn weights in the same project can feel strange at first. The lighter CC yarn creates delicate lace, while the thicker MC adds durability.
- If your lace motifs appear different in size, check your tension as you create each one. Consistency is key.
- Remember that cotton yarn does not have the same stretch as acrylic. Take breaks to avoid hand fatigue.

## How to Customize This Pattern
You can personalize your bucket bag! Choose different color combinations:
- Try burgundy and cream for a fall-like vibe.
- A navy and white combination feels bright and nautical.
- You could even use variegated yarn for either section to add interest without needing to change the pattern proportions.

For a larger bag, continue increasing the base to 72 or 84 stitches before starting the BLO round. Adjust the number of motifs proportionally.
