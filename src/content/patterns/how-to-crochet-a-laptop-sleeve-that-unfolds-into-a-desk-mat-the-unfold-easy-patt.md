## What You'll Need
- **Yarn:**  
  - **Worsted Weight (Category 4)** — this medium-thickness yarn is easy to work with and see your stitches.  
    - **Charcoal (Color A):** approximately 300 yards for the main body.
    - **Light Gray (Color B):** approximately 150 yards for the border.
    - **Pale Blue (Color C):** approximately 100 yards for the flap.
- **Crochet Hook:** 5.5 mm (I-9) — this size works well with worsted weight yarn.
- **Stitch Markers (optional but helpful):** These help keep track of your rows and stitches.
- **Scissors:** For cutting your yarn at the end of each piece.
- **Yarn/Tapestry Needle:** This is used for weaving in the ends after you finish crocheting.
- **Small Button (optional, for closure):** This is to secure the flap down if you choose to use one.

## Stitches You'll Use in This Pattern
- **ch (chain):** Create a little loop that starts your work. To make a chain, hold the yarn in your hand, wrap it around your hook, pull it through the loop on your hook. Repeat to create additional chains.
- **dc (double crochet):** Yarn over (wrap the yarn around your hook), insert your hook into the stitch, yarn over again, pull through. You will have three loops on your hook. Yarn over, pull through the first two loops, yarn over again, pull through the last two loops.
- **sl st (slip stitch):** Insert your hook into the stitch, yarn over, pull through both the stitch and the loop on your hook.
- **st(s) (stitch(es)):** This just refers to the individual stitches you create in your piece.
- **sc (single crochet):** Insert your hook into the stitch, yarn over, pull through. You will have two loops on your hook. Yarn over again, pull through both loops on your hook.
- **tr (treble crochet):** Yarn over twice, insert your hook into the stitch, yarn over, pull through (you will have four loops on your hook). Yarn over, pull through the first two loops. Yarn over, pull through the next two loops. Yarn over, pull through the last two loops.

## Let's Get Started: Full Pattern Instructions

### Body
#### Foundation
1. Using Color A, ch 42. (You can chain 41 for a tighter fit for smaller laptops.)

#### Row 1
2. In the 4th ch from the hook, make 1 dc (double crochet). 
    - You should now have 1 dc and 39 chains remaining on your hook.
3. Now, dc in each ch across the row. 
    - You will have 40 dc when you reach the end. 
    - Turn your work.

#### Row 2
4. Ch 3. This counts as your first dc for this row.
5. Dc in the next dc and each dc across. 
    - You will have 40 dc at the end of the row. 
    - Turn your work.

#### Rows 3-20
6. For Rows 3 through 20, repeat Row 2. 
    - You will have 40 dc at the end of each row.
    - After Row 20, leave your work as it is; do not turn.

#### Add Border
7. Change to Color B. Ch 1.
8. Sc around the entire perimeter of your piece. 
    - Work 3 sc in each corner to keep the edges smooth. 
    - Join with a sl st to the first sc.
    - There should be approximately 160 sc total. 

#### Create Flap
9. Turn the piece so the long side is facing you.
10. In Color C, ch 1.
11. Sc in each sc across for 20 rows. 
    - You will have 40 sc in each of those 20 rows.

### Unfolding Mechanism
#### Buttonhole (Optional)
12. In Row 10 of the flap, make a buttonhole: 
    - Ch 2, skip the next sc, and sc in the next sc to create your button opening.

## Assembly / Putting It All Together
- **Folding Mechanism:** Fold the top flap down towards the back of the piece and use a button to secure it if desired.
- **Weaving in Ends:** Use your tapestry needle to weave in all loose ends to create a clean finish.
- **Blocking:** Optionally, you can block your piece. Blocking is stretching your crochet to shape it and smooth out any unevenness, especially when you used different yarn colors. You can pin it to a flat surface to keep the shape while it dries.

---

## Finishing
- **Weave in Ends:** Use your yarn/tapestry needle to carefully weave in the loose ends from your yarn. Go through a few stitches to secure it and then cut off the excess yarn.
- **Blocking (optional):** If your project feels curled or uneven, to block, dampen it slightly and lay it flat on a clean surface. Use pins to secure the edges in place for a perfectly shaped final piece.
